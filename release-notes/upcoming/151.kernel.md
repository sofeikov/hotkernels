`np.fmax.reduceat(x, starts)` on a one-dimensional `float64` array with `int64` starts is served by a kernel: on runs of 16 elements with 5% NaN it is about 11x faster than NumPy at a million elements, measured on x86-64. The gain is on short runs; on runs of a thousand elements or more in large arrays it can be slower than NumPy. `np.fmax.accumulate` on `float64` is faster too (about 5x at a million elements, was 3.3x), and neither call now leaves the floating-point invalid flag set.
